# Interface review and redesign

The first interface worked, but it was organized around *data* (one tab per table) rather than around *the analyst's job*. This document records the review findings and what changed. The business logic did not change: every rule is still enforced in the service layer, and the automated tests still pass.

## Who uses it and what they need

| Person (simulated role) | Main job | What they need from the screen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Risk Ops analyst | Triage the queue, decide severity and route, investigate | See what is most urgent; compare the AI claim with the evidence; decide in one place |
| Incident Lead | P0/P1 containment approval, closure sign-off, rule governance | Only the approvals that need them; the blockers to closing |
| Safety / Legal-Privacy specialist | Review drafts and policy questions flagged for them | Find the draft waiting on them and approve or reject it |
| Support / Engineering | File reports, add evidence and notes | A clear intake form; an easy way to add evidence |

## Problems found

| # | Problem | Why it hurts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The AI recommendation, the evidence and the decision form were in three different tabs | The core triage decision required constant tab-switching and memory |
| 2 | Opening a case meant choosing an ID in a dropdown and then pressing a button | Slow; easy to open the wrong case |
| 3 | Nothing said *what the case needs next* or *who can do it* | Analysts had to know the state machine by heart |
| 4 | Buttons the current role could not use still looked clickable and failed after the click | Trial-and-error; looked broken |
| 5 | Developer settings (provider mode, database reset) sat beside navigation | Clutter; risk of accidental reset |
| 6 | Raw internal values everywhere (`ASSESSMENT_FAILED`, `legal_privacy`, raw audit rows) | Hard to read under time pressure |
| 7 | The queue was a wide table of 15 columns with horizontal scrolling and no sensible default sort | Urgent items were not obviously first |
| 8 | AI facts listed evidence IDs only | You couldn't check a claim without leaving the page |
| 9 | Messages appeared at the top of the page, far from the control that caused them | Easy to miss errors |
| 10 | Forms (closure, drafts, evidence) were long and inline, and tab state reset after submitting | Lost place; long scrolling |
| 11 | The intake form used a pipe-delimited text box for evidence | Error-prone |

## What changed

**Navigation.** The sidebar has two groups, *Operations* (Queue, Case, New report) and *Oversight* (Dashboard, Quality & evaluation, Rules & playbooks). The simulated identity sits at the top and is labeled "not a login". Provider mode and reset are tucked into a collapsed *Demo settings* panel, and reset needs a confirmation. Case pages have linkable URLs (`/case?id=INC-1002`).

**Queue (problems 2, 7).**
* Views: *Needs action* (the default), *My cases*, *All open*, *Closed*, *All*.
* Search, plus a *Filters* popover.
* Eight readable columns, sorted most urgent first (overdue, then severity, then deadline).
* A *Next action* column that says when the action belongs to another role.
* **Click a row to open the case.**

**Case page (problems 1, 3, 4, 8, 10).** One screen:
* **Header:** severity with its basis ("P0 · confirmed" or "P1 · AI · unconfirmed"), readable status, owner, first-review deadline, AI source, and a mandatory-review flag. Below it, an eight-stage progress bar for the framework's stages.
* **"Next" panel:** generated by `workflow.next_actions()`. It lists what the case needs and marks each item *you can do this* or *needs Incident Lead*, and so on.
* **Left, the case file:**
  * *Overview*: the report, the key facts as a grid with unknowns highlighted, and the AI assessment. The AI's recommendation sits beside the recommendation after safety controls, with the reason for any change.
  * Each AI fact is shown **next to the text of the evidence it cites**. A missing reference is marked in red, and one click records whether the evidence supports the fact.
  * *Evidence*, *Activity* (a readable feed with the raw audit log available below), *Related* and *AI details*.
* **Right, the actions:** severity decision (it switches to "Record override" and asks for a reason as soon as your choice differs from the AI), owner and stage buttons, containment, communications, and closure.
  * Controls the current role may not use are **disabled, with the reason shown**.
  * Closure blockers are listed before the Close button.
  * Closure, draft review, adding evidence and reopening open in **dialogs**.

**Feedback (problem 9).** Successes appear as toasts. Errors appear right under the control that caused them.

**Language (problem 6).** Plain labels throughout: "Awaiting triage", "Legal/Privacy", "Raised to the rules minimum", and so on. The activity feed reads as sentences ("Sam — Incident Lead approved containment … [simulated]").

**Intake (problem 11).** Grouped sections: *What happened*, *Where and who*, *What the assistant did*, *Risk factors*. Evidence is entered in an editable table. JSON import is a separate section.

**Dashboard.** The quality alert sits at the top, followed by KPIs and sections that each answer one question: timeliness, human oversight, issue categories (counts), and AI assessment health.

## Still limited (Streamlit prototype)

* No real authentication or per-user inboxes. "My cases" relies on the simulated owner field.
* The action panel does not stay fixed while the case file scrolls.
* No real-time updates when two people work on the same case; the page refreshes on the next interaction.
* Accessibility has only been checked informally (visible labels, text alongside every colored badge). There has been no screen-reader audit.
* The interface has not been tested with real users; see [timed_review_protocol.md](timed_review_protocol.md).

## Verification

* `scripts/ui_walkthrough.py` drives the demo through the redesigned interface in headless Chromium. It asserts that **a Safety specialist can confirm an automatic pause**, that **Risk Ops cannot approve P0 containment**, and that **the Incident Lead cannot approve a draft flagged for Legal/Privacy** (both controls are disabled). It produced `docs/screenshots/01–13`.
* `scripts/ui_smoke.py` opens every page and every case section and checks that none raises an exception.
* `tests/test_workflow.py::test_next_actions_guide_by_status_and_role` and `::test_closure_blockers_match_close_incident_checks` cover the new guidance services.
